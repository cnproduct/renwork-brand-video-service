# Video knowledge quality gates

## Gate A: source, scope, rights, and privacy

The exact source exists and is stable enough to inspect. Tenant and brand profile come from trusted project context. Rights and intended use are known. Personal, customer, account, credential, and cross-tenant data are minimized or redacted. Extraction is distinguishable from generated interpretation.

## Gate B: claims and terminology

Every material claim has provenance, evidence state, applicability, and risk. Names, numbers, dates, units, prices, product terms, buyer evidence, and CTA are checked. High-risk claims have current authoritative evidence, review date, prohibited overstatement, and a named human reviewer.

## Gate C: subtitle text

Every subtitle in scope is mapped to the correct scene and artifact. Original and proposed text are reviewed sentence by sentence. Meaning changes and unresolved evidence are explicit. The reviewed batch is approved before regeneration.

## Gate D: render integrity

The final file has expected streams, duration, resolution, frame rate, time base, audio, subtitle coverage, and source mapping. There are no unexplained black frames, freezes, truncation, stale timelines, or duplicated source ranges.

## Gate E: audiovisual acceptance

A reviewer checks the beginning, every scene boundary, subtitle-dense sections, ending, logo, brand profile, cover, audio, final frame, and picture-subtitle-audio synchronization in a real player. Component checks do not replace final playback.

## Gate F: reusable pattern

The exact immutable artifact is human accepted, linked to its manifest, and supported by QA evidence. The candidate states one pattern, applicability, parameters when known, scope limits, and forward-test result. A score or generated status alone fails this gate.

## Gate G: state reporting

Report these independently: source available, parsed, generated, structurally validated, audiovisually accepted, staged as knowledge, approved as knowledge, synchronized to repository, deployed, published, destination verified, and measured as a business result.
